How a detection becomes a ranked result

Three stages, each doing one job. You can read every verdict they reach in findings.json. For the whole product in everyday words, read the full guide (PDF).

  1. Detectors report everything they see

    Each detector reads one kind of input and reports every possible use of cryptography, noisily on purpose. Nothing is filtered at this stage.

  2. Verification keeps what the evidence supports

    Each detection gets a confidence from its evidence: a call outweighs a name, and a second kind of evidence raises it. Above the threshold (0.6 by default) a detection is accepted. Below it, it is held back. Known false patterns are rejected, and lines you mark with an acxelin:ignore comment are suppressed.

  3. Ranking orders what to migrate first

    Accepted detections are grouped into assets. Each asset gets a tier from critical to ok, from its algorithm, its key size, whether it protects data that must stay secret, and your timeline.

Your timeline sets the priority

An asset is urgent when the years its data must stay secret, plus the years your migration takes, run past the years until a quantum computer can break it. This is Mosca's inequality. You set the three numbers.

SettingOptionDefault
Years the data must stay secret--shelf-life10
Years your migration will take--migration5
Year a cryptographically relevant quantum computer exists--crqc-year2035

Standards it checks

--policy nist-ir-8547
Flags quantum-vulnerable public-key cryptography with NIST's dates (deprecated after 2030, disallowed after 2035), and anything below 112 bits today.
--policy cnsa-2.0
Checks the CNSA 2.0 suite: AES-256, SHA-384 and SHA-512, ML-KEM-1024 and ML-DSA-87. A key size that was not observed is reported as unverified, never as compliant.
Your own rules
Forbid algorithms, set minimum key sizes and require a hybrid key exchange in a [policy] table in .acxelin-readiness.toml.

Where the evidence ends

  • Static analysis shows what your files say, not what runs in production.
  • A suppression comment or usedforsecurity=False is the developer's statement, and the report marks it as one.
  • The precision and recall figures from the project's labelled test corpus guard against regressions. They are not a claim about code the scanner has never seen.
