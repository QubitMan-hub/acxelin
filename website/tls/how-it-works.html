How a connection is decided

The edge's policy is checked on every connection. Nothing falls back silently: a connection that cannot meet the policy ends, and the log says why. For the whole product in everyday words, read the full guide (PDF).

  1. Key exchange

    Under strict, the client must offer a post-quantum group: X25519MLKEM768, SecP384r1MLKEM1024 or SecP256r1MLKEM768. A client that offers only classical groups is refused, and the log says the client offers no post-quantum key exchange.

  2. Certificates

    Under strict, certificates must use ML-DSA. A client that cannot verify ML-DSA is refused, and the log says so.

  3. Client certificate

    With require_client_cert, the client must present a certificate that chains to your authority and is not on its revocation list. Without a current list, the edge refuses everyone rather than let a revoked client in.

The certificate authority

Create
acxelin-tls ca init makes a root (ML-DSA-87 by default) in a folder: its certificate, its key (encrypted unless you ask otherwise), an index of everything it issued, and the revocation list.
Issue and renew
Certificates are ML-DSA-65 by default and last 397 days. ca issue makes a key and certificate, ca renew reissues, and ca sign-csr certifies a key made elsewhere.
Revoke
ca revoke SERIAL marks the certificate revoked and signs a new list. The list is valid for 7 days, so run ca maintain daily to keep it fresh.
Reach the edges
ca publish serves the root certificate and the list over HTTP. An edge on another machine follows the list with crl_url and keeps only one the authority signed, that has not expired and is not older than its copy.

The edge

An edge is one or more routes. terminate puts post-quantum TLS in front of a plain service. originate lets a plain local client reach a remote edge. Changing the configuration file reloads it without a restart, and certificates are re-read when their files change.

With --metrics, an edge serves /healthz, /metrics for Prometheus and /status as JSON: open connections, handshakes accepted and refused by reason, and connections by negotiated key-exchange group.
