How a tunnel gets its keys

The same four steps for a branch site and for a person's laptop. A peer that fails any of them gets no keys, and nothing falls back to a key exchange a quantum computer could break.

  1. Mutual TLS

    One side connects to the other's key agreement service, on port 7443, over TLS 1.3 with a hybrid ML-KEM group: X25519MLKEM768, SecP384r1MLKEM1024 or SecP256r1MLKEM768. Both present ML-DSA certificates from your authority, and both are checked against its revocation list. A classical client, a certificate from elsewhere or the wrong kind of certificate is refused.

  2. Keys from the session

    Both sides derive the same secrets from that TLS session with an exporter (RFC 5705), bound to both names and a fresh tag. For IPsec that is the IKE pre-shared key and a post-quantum PPK (RFC 8784) with its id; for WireGuard, the pre-shared key. The keys themselves never cross the network.

  3. Rotation

    Sites agree new keys every hour by default, people's computers every two minutes. A side that stops re-agreeing is removed after three missed rotations, so stale keys do not linger.

  4. Revocation

    A revoked certificate gets no new keys. Every 15 seconds the gateway checks the certificates it has given keys to, and cuts the tunnel of any that is revoked. Without a current revocation list it refuses everyone.

Site to site

strongSwan 6.1 carries the traffic. Acxelin VPN loads each agreed key into it, sets the IKE proposal, and starts or replaces the tunnel. Both ends of a tunnel use the same profile.

Remote access

The WireGuard gateway hands each person an address from its pool and routes the networks behind it. With full_tunnel, all of a laptop's traffic goes through the gateway and the laptop gets a kill switch that blocks traffic outside the tunnel while protection is down. The gateway can limit access to a list of names, or let in anyone with a client certificate from your authority.

What the gateways report

With metrics set, a site and a WireGuard gateway serve /metrics for Prometheus and /status as JSON, which the console reads.

MetricMeaning
acxelin_vpn_tunnel_up1 while the site's tunnel to that peer is installed
acxelin_vpn_bytes_totaltraffic through each tunnel, in and out
acxelin_vpn_events_totalkey agreements, refusals and revoked peers

acxelin-vpn inventory writes what the VPN uses as a CycloneDX 1.6 CBOM, for an audit: tunnels, key exchanges, ciphers, certificates, and whether a PSK and PPK are present, by type and size, never a value.
